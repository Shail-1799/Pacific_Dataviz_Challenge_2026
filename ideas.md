# Design directions — Smallest Emitters, Biggest Losers

## Three directions considered

| Theme Name | Very Brief Intro | Probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oceanic Ledger** | An editorial investigation rendered like a climate-debt balance sheet, using deep ocean ink, paper-like surfaces, and sharp coral-red loss marks. It makes the injustice feel evidentiary rather than decorative. | 0.04 |
| **Tidal Evidence** | A sunlit, cartographic Pacific atlas that treats each island as a data witness, combining maritime field notes with restrained scientific plotting. It creates warmth and place without resorting to tourism imagery. | 0.07 |
| **Redline Horizon** | A high-contrast, poster-like visual protest in charcoal and safety orange, where emissions and impacts are drawn as opposing pressure lines. It is intentionally urgent and confrontational. | 0.02 |

## Chosen direction: Oceanic Ledger

### Design Movement

**Editorial data journalism meets climate-accountability dossier.** The experience should feel like a public-interest investigation with the interactive confidence of a contemporary long-form data story.

### Core Principles

1. **Evidence before spectacle.** Every major visual must make a defensible comparison clear within seconds.
2. **Asymmetry is the story.** The interaction and layout should visibly exaggerate the disparity between a small emissions footprint and a large disaster burden without distorting quantitative scales.
3. **Place remains visible.** Pacific countries are named, selectable, and never collapsed into an anonymous regional dot.
4. **Transparency earns emotion.** Units, years, source coverage, caveats, and data gaps remain accessible alongside the argument.

### Color Philosophy

Deep ocean ink signals the Pacific as a lived environment and creates a calm, investigative field for data. Warm parchment panels introduce the feel of an annotated ledger. Coral is reserved for losses and harms; sea-glass teal represents the emissions footprint. Gold only flags a selected country or key interaction state. The contrast makes the moral imbalance legible without visual melodrama.

### Layout Paradigm

An **asymmetric dossier** rather than a dashboard. A full-width claim occupies the upper field; the central scatterplot is the evidence board; a narrow evidence rail contains country controls, metrics, and methodological notes. The lower half becomes a selected-country case file and an explorable ranking rather than a grid of equal cards.

### Signature Elements

1. A **carbon-to-consequence scatter field** with a diagonal “more emissions” / “more harm” reading and highlighted Pacific cases.
2. A **loss ledger** in which fine horizontal rules and red impact bars resemble audited entries.
3. A subtle **tide-line texture** in the hero/background, used only as atmosphere and never behind dense text.

### Interaction Philosophy

Controls should feel like examining evidence: choose a reporting period, metric, and country; hover to reveal exact values and source coverage; click a Pacific country to open its mini case file. The story should be useful at first glance but reward investigation.

### Animation

On initial load, data points rise into their final positions from the bottom edge over 280ms with a short stagger, then remain stable. Selection should use opacity and a narrow coral outline rather than dramatic bouncing. Hover transitions remain under 160ms. All nonessential motion is disabled for users who prefer reduced motion.

### Typography System

**DM Serif Display** carries the main claim and selected-country names with journalistic gravity. **Source Sans 3** carries controls, chart labels, and methods for compact legibility. Headlines use sentence case with a measured editorial rhythm; numeric summaries use tabular figures.

### Brand Essence

**A public-interest climate ledger for seeing why the Pacific pays for a crisis it scarcely caused.**

Personality: **forensic, compassionate, unflinching**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines should name the imbalance plainly; CTAs should invite scrutiny rather than sell a product; microcopy should distinguish evidence from interpretation.

Example lines: “The carbon ledger is not where the losses land.”

Example lines: “Choose a country. Read the imbalance in full.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The mark is a compact **split tide-ring**: one thin teal arc, one heavier coral arc, separated like an imbalanced balance scale. It has no text and will be used beside the wordmark and as the favicon.

### Signature Brand Color

**Ledger Coral — #E46F61.**

## Style Decisions

The central evidence view uses a pale parchment surface rather than a dark dashboard. Coral is restricted to disaster burden and selected states; emissions are consistently sea-glass teal. No generic dashboard cards, dense control bars, or novelty visual metaphors will displace the core scatterplot.

# Competition rebuild: The Pacific Signal Room

## Chosen direction: Signal Room

### Design Movement

**Oceanographic instrument panel meets public-interest field journal.** The rebuilt interactive will not resemble a dashboard or a climate-debt scatterplot. It will feel like entering a Pacific observation room where changing sea, warming water, and the visible monitoring record are examined together.

### Core Principles

1. **Official data at the center.** The story is driven by the 2026 official Pacific Data Hub indicators, not by supporting context.
2. **A human question, not a generic ranking.** The interface asks whether a changing ocean is being observed in time—not which country is “winning” or “losing.”
3. **A visible uncertainty boundary.** Monitoring-network counts are presented as records of reported network sites, not as total early-warning capacity, traditional knowledge, or a proxy for resilience.
4. **One memorable visual grammar.** Signals emerge as lines, pulses, and quiet instruments across a Pacific field instead of default cards and charts.

### Color Philosophy

Black-blue **Signal Night** makes the data feel monitored rather than decorative. Phosphor seafoam represents observed ocean signals; vermilion marks elevated change; low-luminance amber identifies the monitoring record. Paper is reserved for explanations and methods, creating a deliberate contrast between what is observed and how it is interpreted.

### Layout Paradigm

An **instrument narrative**: a full-screen Pacific signal field starts the story; a central explorer lets the visitor tune what is being observed; an evidence strip exposes four official indicators across places; a selected-place “field log” explains the historical trace. The information flows from regional signal to local evidence, not from a conventional hero into a dashboard.

### Signature Elements

1. A **Signal Constellation**: country/territory nodes positioned as a Pacific-inspired field, with rings for latest sea-surface anomaly and arcs for the reported monitoring count.
2. A **tunable signal rail** that shifts the field between warming water, sea-level change, monitoring record, and official GHG-per-capita context.
3. A **field-log trace** that layers a selected place’s time series and makes the earliest/latest record explicit.

### Interaction Philosophy

Every interaction is framed as tuning or listening: select a signal, choose a place, then read the recorded evidence. No interaction may imply causal certainty. Motion should reveal sequence and attention, never fake scientific precision.

### Animation

Nodes resolve from faint to legible in 480ms with irregular 40–70ms delays, simulating instruments coming online. The selected node sends a single low-opacity ripple into the field. Metric changes cross-fade over 180ms; users who prefer reduced motion see the final state immediately.

### Typography System

**DM Serif Display** remains for question-led headlines and place names. **Source Sans 3** carries the technical field-log language. A monospaced numeric treatment is used only for the latest-year evidence, creating the feel of a measured observation without turning the page into a terminal.

### Brand Essence

**A Pacific climate observation room that asks whether the places living with a changing ocean can see the signal in time.**

Personality: **attentive, precise, quietly urgent**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines ask clear questions; controls use listening and observation vocabulary; footnotes make limits visible.

Example lines: “The ocean is changing. What can we see from shore?”

Example lines: “This is a record of monitoring sites—not a measure of all local knowledge.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The mark becomes an **open signal ring**: three interrupted seafoam arcs, interrupted by one vermilion pulse. It suggests a receiving instrument and an incomplete signal, with no text.

### Signature Brand Color

**Signal Seafoam — #8FD8D4.**

## Style Decisions

All summary-number surfaces act as calibrated instruments, with traces, ticks, interruption marks, and record labels rather than generic KPI-card styling. Signal Seafoam remains the ambient field color and the base grammar of every data surface; amber only denotes monitoring-record values, while vermilion marks an interruption, elevated change, or selected pulse. The open signal-ring motif repeats as interrupted arcs and a single pulse throughout the field, trace, and selected state.

The first viewport carries a faint but legible monitoring field—interrupted rings, pulse marks, and traces—to establish the Signal Room before the visitor reaches the explorer. The open-ring wordmark lockup reinforces the brand name without competing with the data question. The author profile intentionally retains the user-requested bracketed placeholders until the entrant supplies their own attribution; no invented identity, organization, email, or contact link will replace them.

Paper field-journal sections retain the Signal Room grammar through faint ruled-record grids, calibration marks, and interrupted-ring traces. The four signal summaries read as measured instrument surfaces—each has record dates, calibration ticks, a trace cue, and a signal ring—rather than generic KPI cards. Vermilion remains reserved for selected nodes, interruptions, and elevated-change signals; seafoam carries the ambient system throughout.

The first viewport reads as an active monitoring field before scrolling: interrupted seafoam rings, paired trace lines, calibrated marks, and a single vermilion pulse remain visible around the record summary. Paper and source sections carry ruled-record grids and open-ring evidence dividers so the field-journal grammar does not become generic report-card styling. The three-interruption open signal ring plus one vermilion pulse recurs at hero, wall, context, selected state, and source boundaries.

### Evidence callouts, night reading, and exports

Fixed annotations use a restrained **field-note callout**: an open seafoam or amber tag, a single leader line with an arrowhead, an exact official value/year, and—where necessary—a short interpretation boundary. A trace may show no more than two fixed callouts: the first and latest record. The callout offsets alternate above and below the line and are clamped inside the plot, preventing label collisions while retaining the on-demand tooltip for every annual mark. In the signal field, only the currently selected node receives a leader annotation; all country names remain part of the schematic rather than competing labels.

The appearance toggle is a **reading-light control**, not a generic theme switch. The existing ink-and-phosphor room remains the night-reading state; the alternate state converts paper sections to a cool, low-glare blue-black field journal with high-contrast chalk text while retaining seafoam, amber, and vermilion meanings. It persists the visitor’s preference and never changes the data encoding.

Night-reading values and labels must be explicitly declared against their actual dark instrument surface rather than relying on inherited paper-field text. Selected values use warm chalk `#fffdf4` at 700 weight; place labels use pale seafoam `#d8fff8` at 650 weight; year labels use `#b9e3dc` at 600 weight. No dark-ink inherited rule may override these fields. The Wall of Signals always reserves a solid translucent reading plate behind its place and value labels in night mode so texture cannot reduce contrast.

The **60-second judge tour** is a voluntary evidence route, not autoplay theatre. Its sequence is: the official-record overview; the tunable field and its “not a resilience score” boundary; the Wall’s exact SST reading; one endpoint annotation in a selected trace; the official SDG 11 consequence coverage caveat; and the source dossier / evidence export. A single focus ring and concise overlay label move to the current evidence target. Visitors can pause, go back, skip, or use the existing page normally; reduced-motion users receive instantaneous focus changes.

Loading feedback treats embedded official data as a calibrated instrument coming online: short, reduced-motion-aware skeleton bars and a source-specific status line appear for the SDG 11 consequence register without suggesting a live network fetch. The remaining export paths are concise evidence packets: visible source, unit, date range, selected place, and caveat travel with each PNG, PDF, or filtered CSV download.

### Full judge-readiness roadmap decisions

The expanded experience begins with a **Signal Brief** that asks one of three entry questions and describes the currently selected official record without calling any place representative, extreme, or most affected. A compact **coverage curtain** uses availability bars and blank space—not a heat scale—to distinguish the scope of the core, consequence, and verified rainfall records. The live **Evidence Passport** behaves like a field calibration slip: it exposes the current location, dataflow, indicator, units, period, availability, and reading limit, and is copyable through a stable URL fragment.

The reading path stays deliberately calm for first-time visitors: only language, reading light, and a collapsible **Room controls** dock appear in the primary header. The dock retains tours, sources, sharing, exports, and the record jump without competing with the central question. Every paper-field-journal surface carries at least one quiet Signal Room primitive—ruled record lines, calibration ticks, a trace fragment, a source stamp, or an interrupted-ring divider. The open signal-ring seal becomes a repeated source-and-evidence anchor; vermilion remains reserved for selection, interruption, elevated change, and documented consequence.

New annotations retain the field-note grammar: no more than three visitor pins, thin leader arrows, exact values, and a selected-state reading board. Citation-safe **Field Note postcards** are not campaign graphics; each layout preserves a source and caveat. A calm source-to-mark sequence visualises only the real documented path from official extract to transformed record to on-screen mark. The atlas’s new spatial-unit key always distinguishes EEZ-scale ocean records, place-reported official records, and station/network counts without pretending one is more precise or local than another.

Verified rainfall lives in a separate rain register with an **MM** unit label and its own scale; it never shares a y-axis or colour meaning with ocean temperature. The Annual Shorelines (Landsat, 30 m) resource appears only as a clearly bounded source card because the catalogue requires access approval for full data; it is not treated as an embedded observed impact layer. A final community-material slot is visibly unfilled until a real, licensed contribution with permission can be added.
