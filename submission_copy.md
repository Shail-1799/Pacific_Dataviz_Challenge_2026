## Title

**Smallest Emitters, Biggest Losers**

## Format

**Interactive dataviz — public URL to be inserted after the project is published.**

## Problem addressed

Climate change is often described through global emissions totals and regional averages. Those summaries can obscure an inequity that is central to Pacific climate justice: disaster burdens land in countries and territories whose contribution to global greenhouse-gas emissions is tiny. At the same time, “small emitters” and “low emitters per person” are not identical claims. A defensible climate-justice story should let people inspect both measures, rather than flattening them into a slogan.

## How the dataviz responds

**Smallest Emitters, Biggest Losers** is an interactive Pacific climate-justice ledger. Its main scatterplot pairs greenhouse-gas emissions with reported direct disaster burden for each country or territory with comparable source coverage. Users can switch the horizontal measure between greenhouse-gas emissions per person and share of global greenhouse-gas emissions; they can switch the vertical measure between direct economic loss as a share of GDP and people affected per 1,000 residents. Bubble size maintains the people-affected comparison in either view.

The country case file, annual impact trace, and official Pacific Data Hub sea-surface-temperature anomaly context make the comparison inspectable rather than merely rhetorical. The experience does not claim that a specific temperature anomaly directly caused a particular disaster impact. It uses the Pacific Data Hub series to contextualize the changing ocean alongside the impact record.

## Method

The main comparison uses annual observations from 2000 to 2024. The displayed country means are calculated across the years reported by each source. The app displays the number of years with reported disaster-loss and people-affected observations and does not convert source gaps into zeroes.

Economic damages are direct losses reported by EM-DAT and shown as a percentage of annual GDP. People affected is the EM-DAT total of people injured, requiring immediate assistance, or made homeless. The app derives each country’s share of global greenhouse-gas emissions from the same OWID greenhouse-gas-per-capita series multiplied by population and divided by the corresponding World total.

## Data sources

1. **Per capita greenhouse gas emissions including land use.** Jones et al. (2025); Population based on various sources (2024) – with major processing by Our World in Data. https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/per-capita-ghg-emissions
2. **Annual economic damages from all natural disasters as a share of GDP.** EM-DAT, CRED / UCLouvain (2026); National statistical organizations and central banks, OECD national accounts, and World Bank staff estimates (2026) – with major processing by Our World in Data. https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/natural-disasters-economic-damages
3. **Annual number of people affected by all natural disasters.** EM-DAT, CRED / UCLouvain (2026) – with major processing by Our World in Data. https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/natural-disasters-people-affected
4. **Sea Surface Temperature anomalies.** SPC Pacific Data Hub `.Stat`, Climate Change indicators, `DF_CLIMATE_CHANGE`, `SST_ANOM`, annual data 2017–2025. https://stats-nsi-stable.pacificdata.org/rest/data/DF_CLIMATE_CHANGE

## Short description

An interactive Pacific climate-justice ledger that makes a hard question visible: why do reported disaster losses and people affected concentrate far from the global greenhouse-gas ledger?
