# Signal Room judge-readiness research

## Official 2026 challenge requirements confirmed

The Pacific Dataviz Challenge 2026 runs from 1 June to 31 August. It accepts an interactive dataviz in the main competition, requires at least one dataset from the annual official list, permits additional open data, and requires the submission to be public. The main competition is open to individuals and teams, including international entrants.[1]

The 2026 judging panel named on the Challenge site spans Pacific climate policy and sustainability (Coral Pasisi), Pacific affairs (Salā Dr George Carter), ecology and resilience development (Dr Manumatavai Tupou-Roosen), public-interest journalism (Eddie Trevor Osifelo), Pacific climate advocacy (Vishal Prasad), and data-visualisation practice (Yan Holtz).[1]

## Implications for Signal Room

The platform’s current primary official dataflow, `SPC:DF_CLIMATE_CHANGE(1.0)`, is an official listed source. The work should therefore prioritise an authored, comprehensible Pacific story; transparent, reproducible evidence; lively but accessible interaction; and a public, judge-ready reading path. It must not assert causal relationships or resilience/risk rankings beyond the observed and reported official records.

## What the rules do and do not publish

Text review of the complete 11-page rules confirms definitions, eligibility, category structure, use of official and open data, source disclosure, public display, intellectual-property provisions, and jury discretion. It does **not** publish a numeric scoring rubric or weights. The recommendations therefore treat storytelling, design, innovation, technical execution, source integrity, and relevance to Pacific climate issues as well-supported judging priorities from the official Challenge framing and panel composition—not as invented point allocations.[1]
